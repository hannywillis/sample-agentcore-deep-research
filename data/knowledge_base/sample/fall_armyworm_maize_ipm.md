# Recognising fall armyworm
Fall armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda) larvae feed inside the maize whorl. Signs include ragged holes and "window panes" on young leaves and wet, sawdust-like frass in the funnel. Older larvae have an inverted Y mark on the head and four dark spots arranged in a square near the tail.

# Scouting
Walk the field in a W pattern twice a week from emergence until tasselling. Check 10 plants at each of 5 points (50 plants in total). Action is usually recommended when about 20 percent of young plants (before knee height) or about 40 percent of older plants show fresh damage.

# Cultural and biological control
Plant early and at the same time as neighbouring farms so that the crop escapes peak moth flights. Intercropping maize with legumes such as beans or desmodium and keeping field margins with grasses (push-pull) reduce damage. Hand-pick and crush egg masses and young larvae on small plots. Natural enemies such as parasitic wasps and earwigs help; avoid unnecessary broad-spectrum sprays that kill them. Biopesticides based on Bacillus thuringiensis or Metarhizium are options where registered.

# Chemical control
Use only pesticides registered for fall armyworm by the national regulator (in Kenya, the Pest Control Products Board). Apply into the whorl early in the morning or late afternoon when larvae are active, wear protective clothing, follow the label rate and rotate products with different modes of action to slow resistance. Consult an extension officer before spraying, especially on food crops close to harvest.
